Five Adelaide settings that change the same brief

Locality is not decoration when it affects access, exposure or the base under the finish. The source guidance points to distinct settings rather than one citywide template.

  • Apartment: ask how lift booking, entry protection, waste removal and neighbour impact shape the program.
  • Coastal or outdoor: ask how salt, sun, drainage and slip needs will be assessed.
  • Older villa: confirm substrate checks before demolition or tile ordering.
  • Newer slab: ask how movement joints and adjoining transitions will be set out.
  • Mixed renovation: state which trades touch the room and when each handover occurs.

Wet areas: question the layer behind the finish

A splashback and a shower should not attract the same level of scrutiny. For wet areas, tiles and grout are surfaces; the membrane, penetrations, thresholds and falls sit behind them.

  • Request membrane boundaries, sealant around penetrations, threshold heights and the handover point to tiling.
  • Separate visible signs from faults still needing inspection; leaks may involve sealant, plumbing, movement or the membrane system.
  • Do not accept regrouting as a universal fix before diagnosis.
  • Treat cure time as product, film thickness and site specific, not a promised wait period.

Make quotes comparable without changing facts

Send one brief to each provider and keep the variables constant. Then variation in price is more likely to show method, risk allocation or exclusions rather than a different guess.

  1. Describe the area: room, surface, symptoms, fixtures, drainage and moisture.
  2. Split assumptions: plumbing, substrate, waterproofing, screed and finish are separate decisions.
  3. List inclusions, exclusions, access, waste and inspection items in writing.
  4. Ask who confirms scope, applicable licence or authorisation where required in South Australia, products used, and how plumbing or electrical coordination is recorded.

A pre-acceptance sequence you can reuse

Before acceptance, move from evidence to paperwork. Confirm demolition, rough-ins, membrane application, cure, set-out and handover in the written plan. If waterproofing, screeding, demolition, coordination, hazardous-material questions, tile supply, waste or access is silent, ask whether it is included, excluded or subject to inspection. Keep product names and the recorded system with the quote, and verify licensing and specifications with the qualified provider responsible for the work.

  1. Document. Record room, suburb, surface, symptoms, fixtures, drainage and visible moisture.
  2. Split layers. List plumbing, substrate, waterproofing, screed and finish assumptions separately.
  3. Issue one brief. Send identical information to each provider and ask for comparable line items.
  4. Test silence. Where scope is unstated, ask if it is included, excluded or inspection-dependent.
  5. Accept in writing. Confirm sequencing, products, licensed work, cure responsibility and exclusions before work starts.

Key takeaway

Before seeking prices, record the room, suburb or postcode, existing surface, symptoms, fixtures that may move, drainage details and any visible moisture. Treat tiles and grout as finishes over plumbing, substrate, waterproofing, screed and movement decisions. Ask where membrane responsibility starts and ends, how cure sequencing is recorded, and which items are included, excluded or inspection-dependent. Local setting, apartment access, coastal exposure, older villas and newer slabs can change preparation and order of work.

Common questions

Can square metres alone price a bathroom fairly? No. The guidance warns that bathrooms, laundries, kitchens, living spaces and alfresco areas carry different preparation, drainage, access and moisture demands, so area should sit beside condition and scope.

Is regrouting enough for a leaking shower? Not automatically. Moisture may be associated with sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system, so diagnosis should come before a repair is sold.

What should be confirmed before acceptance? Confirm who owns scope, relevant licence or authorisation in South Australia, products, trade coordination, cure sequencing, and how exclusions or inspections are handled.
